How to Identify a Suspicious Message

Phishing is a technique in which someone attempts to obtain information by pretending to be a legitimate person, company or service. A phishing message can arrive through SMS, email, social media or messaging apps.

Check the message before clicking

Start by considering why you received the message. Be cautious if an unexpected message claims that your account will immediately be blocked, that you have won a prize, or that you must make a payment urgently.

Look carefully at links

A displayed company name does not necessarily mean that the link belongs to that company. Before opening a link, inspect its address carefully. Misspellings, unusual domains and shortened links deserve additional attention.

Never share sensitive authentication information

Treat passwords, one-time passwords, PINs and recovery information as confidential. A legitimate service may provide instructions through its official application or website rather than requiring you to disclose an authentication code to another person.

When uncertain

Open the official website or application yourself instead of using the link supplied in the suspicious message. This reduces the possibility of accidentally visiting a fraudulent page.

How to Create a Basic Phone Backup

A phone contains contacts, photographs, messages, documents and other information that may be difficult to replace if the device is lost, damaged or reset. A backup provides another copy of important data.

Start with the most important information

Identify information that would be difficult to recreate. This can include family photographs, educational documents, receipts, contacts and work-related files.

Use trusted storage

Depending on the phone and service you use, backups may be stored in a cloud service, on a computer or on another storage device. Choose a service you understand and protect the account with a strong, unique password.

Check that the backup actually exists

Turning on a backup option is not the same as verifying the backup. Periodically check the backup status and available storage. If the service reports an error, resolve it before assuming that the data is protected.

Keep important documents in more than one place

For particularly important files, maintaining another copy can reduce the risk associated with a single device or account failure.

What to Check Before an Online Purchase

Online shopping can be convenient, but a product page should not be treated as the only source of information. A few checks before payment can help you understand the transaction.

Check the seller

Look for the seller or business information provided by the platform. Consider whether the seller details, product information and customer support information are clear.

Read the return conditions

Return policies can differ between products and sellers. Check whether the item is returnable, the permitted time period and whether additional conditions apply.

Review the final price

Before confirming payment, review the product price, delivery charges, taxes or other displayed charges. The final amount should be clear before the transaction is completed.

Keep the transaction record

Save the order confirmation, invoice and relevant communication. These records can be useful if you later need to contact the seller or platform about the transaction.

Protecting Personal Information Online

Personal information can include your name, contact details, photographs, location information and information associated with online accounts. Not every service needs access to all of this data.

Review app permissions

Check which permissions applications have requested. If an application does not need a particular permission for its stated purpose, consider whether that access is necessary.

Use separate passwords

Reusing the same password across multiple services increases the consequences if one account is compromised. Where practical, use different passwords and enable additional account security features.

Be selective when sharing information

Before submitting personal information to a website, consider why it is being requested, whether the website explains how it will be used, and whether the information is actually necessary.

How to Organize Important Digital Documents

A simple folder structure can make digital documents easier to locate. The exact structure depends on your needs, but consistency is more important than complexity.

Use broad categories

  • Education
  • Employment
  • Banking and financial records
  • Receipts and purchases
  • Personal documents
  • Important correspondence

Use understandable filenames

Instead of keeping generic names such as “document1.pdf”, use a meaningful name containing the document type and date when appropriate. This makes searching considerably easier.

Protect sensitive files

Documents containing personal or financial information should not be placed in publicly accessible folders or shared links without checking their access settings.

Reducing Unwanted Calls and Messages

Unwanted calls and messages can be inconvenient and sometimes attempt to direct users toward fraudulent websites or services.

Do not engage with suspicious messages

If a message appears suspicious, avoid clicking links or replying with personal information. Use the reporting and blocking functions available in your messaging or calling application.

Review notification settings

Modern phones provide controls for notifications and communication. Reviewing these settings can reduce interruptions without requiring you to uninstall useful applications.

Keep records of serious incidents

If a communication appears fraudulent, threatening or otherwise significant, retain relevant information such as the date, sender details and message content before deleting or blocking it.

Digital Safety

A simple safety checklist.

Good digital habits are usually more useful than complicated security settings that users do not understand.

Before clicking a link

  • Check who sent the message.
  • Consider whether you expected the message.
  • Inspect the website address.
  • Be careful with urgent payment requests.
  • Do not provide authentication codes to others.

Before installing an app

  • Check the application developer.
  • Review requested permissions.
  • Read the application's description.
  • Keep your operating system updated.
  • Remove applications you no longer use.
